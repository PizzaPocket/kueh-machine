# Product Vision

## Working Title

**Rewind**
*Rediscover the songs, trends and moments that shaped your generation.*

Alternative names:

* Memory.fm
* Soundtrack of Your Life
* BackTrack
* Nostalgia Radio
* Then & Now

---

# Vision

Rewind helps people rediscover forgotten parts of their lives through music, cultural moments, and visual memory triggers.

The internet is excellent at helping people discover new content, but poor at helping people remember content that once mattered deeply to them.

Over the years, the way people consume music has changed dramatically:

* CDs
* Burned discs
* MP3 downloads
* iPods
* Apple Music
* Spotify

As users migrated between platforms, much of their personal music history was lost.

People often remember that music was important to them, but can no longer remember what they listened to, when they listened to it, or why it mattered.

Rewind exists to reconnect people with those forgotten memories.

---

# Problem Statement

People lose access to the music and cultural moments that defined their childhood and teenage years.

Current music platforms optimize for:

* Discovery
* Recommendations
* New releases
* Current listening habits

They do not help users answer questions like:

* What songs did people my age listen to growing up?
* What was popular when I was 14?
* What songs did I probably play on repeat in secondary school?
* What forgotten songs would instantly bring me back to a specific moment in my life?

The result is a lost personal soundtrack.

---

# Product Thesis

People do not miss music.

They miss their relationship with music.

The strongest emotional response does not come from hearing a popular song.

It comes from rediscovering a forgotten song that was once meaningful.

The goal is not to help users find music.

The goal is to help users remember who they were.

---

# Core Experience

A user provides:

* Birth year
* Country or region
* Preferred language(s)

The system generates a personalized nostalgia timeline based on:

* Age
* Geography
* Cultural context
* Historical music popularity

Instead of showing a generic chart, Rewind reconstructs moments from the user's life.

Example:

## Age 14 (2007)

You were in secondary school.

This was the era of:

* MSN Messenger
* Friendster
* Sony Ericsson Walkman phones
* Bluetooth song sharing
* Windows XP computer labs

Popular songs from your generation:

* Umbrella
* Beautiful Girls
* Bad Day
* Welcome to the Black Parade

The experience combines music, imagery, and cultural memories to recreate a forgotten period of life.

### Childhood

### Teenage Years

Ages 13–18

### Young Adult Years

Ages 19–25

Each stage contains:

* Popular songs
* Cultural references
* Trends

* Visual memories

Examples:

### Technology

* Sony Ericsson Walkman
* Nokia phones
* Windows XP
* iPod Nano

### Internet Culture

* MSN Messenger
* Friendster
* MySpace

### Gaming

* MapleStory

### Entertainment

* Popular TV shows
* Movies
* Celebrity culture

---

## 3. Forgotten Gems

The signature feature of the platform.

Forgotten Gems are songs that:

* Were highly popular during a specific period
* Were culturally relevant to a specific audience
* Have largely disappeared from current listening habits

Examples:

Users often remember:

* Umbrella
* Love Story

Users often forget:

* Bad Day
* Beautiful Soul
* Unwritten
* Leave (Get Out)

These forgotten songs often trigger stronger nostalgia than mainstream classics.

Desired reaction:

"How did I forget this song existed?"

---

# Forgotten Gem Framework

A Forgotten Gem Score is calculated using:

## Historical Popularity

How popular the song was during its peak.

Sources:

* Billboard
* Regional charts
* Radio stations
* Music archives

---

## Age Relevance

How old the user was when the song was popular.

Highest weighting:

* Ages 10–25

Lower weighting:

* Ages outside formative years

---

## Regional Relevance

How culturally relevant the song was in the user's country or region.

Example:

A Malaysian teenager in 2007 likely had different music exposure than an American teenager in 2007.

---

## Current Popularity

How actively the song is consumed today.

Lower current popularity increases Forgotten Gem score.

---

## Memory Trigger Score

How strongly a song is associated with a specific cultural moment or life experience.

Examples:

* School life
* First crush
* Gaming
* Family road trips
* Tuition classes
* MSN Messenger era
