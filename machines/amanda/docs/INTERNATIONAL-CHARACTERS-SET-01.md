# International Character Set 01

This handoff covers the next three customers only:

1. Ube Biko from the Philippines
2. Luk Chup from Thailand
3. Pink Songpyeon from Korea

Do not build their chapters or minigames yet. Do not add minigame clues. The stories below are the approved narrative direction for later implementation.

## Shared implementation rules

- Each character has five dialogue sprites: `neutral`, `worried`, `thinking`, `remembering`, and `happy`.
- Keep the fictional emotional story separate from the educational facts.
- Use the worried sprite while the customer explains what feels missing.
- Use the thinking sprite while Beary asks reflective questions.
- Use the remembering sprite at the first clear breakthrough.
- Use the happy sprite only after the memory is fully restored.
- Do not use em dashes in dialogue, card copy, or interface text.
- Keep all card tags short. Use exactly three tags in this order: region or country, food type, defining trait.

## Ube Biko

### Character direction

Ube Biko is warm, thoughtful and a little self-conscious. They worry that their sticky texture makes them troublesome. Their purple colour is bold, but their personality begins quiet and apologetic.

### Story title

The Memory That Stuck

### Full story

Ube Biko arrives at the counter with a few golden grains clinging to their sleeves. Before Beary can greet them, they begin apologising.

They cannot remember where they came from, but one feeling remains very clearly. Everything sticks. Memories stick to one another. Tiny details cling when they are trying to let go. Even the scent of coconut seems to follow them into the café.

Ube Biko worries that this must mean they were difficult to handle. Perhaps they slowed everyone down. Perhaps they made a mess. They look at their deep purple layers and wonder why something so bright should feel so heavy.

Beary asks whether every kind of sticking has to be a burden. Ube Biko grows quiet. A new memory begins to surface: a broad tray in the centre of a table, patient hands stirring rice, coconut warmth rising through the room, and people cutting portions to share.

The memories were not trapped together. They were held together.

Ube Biko remembers that their soft glutinous rice, coconut richness and purple ube were never meant to stand alone. Each part became more comforting when joined with the others. Even the tray belonged to more than one person. It was prepared slowly, sliced carefully and passed from hand to hand.

What Ube Biko had mistaken for messiness was connection. Their memories stayed because people had made them together.

With their memory restored, Ube Biko smiles and says, "Maybe being sticky does not mean I hold people back. Maybe it means I help good things stay close."

Beary realises that some memories survive not because one person holds them tightly, but because many people carry them together.

### Card story

Ube Biko worried that being sticky meant holding everyone back. With Beary's help, they remembered purple rice, coconut warmth and a tray shared among many hands. What once felt messy became proof that some memories stay because they are held together.

### Card tags

- Philippines
- Rice Cake
- Coconut

### Cultural foundation for later fact-checking

Biko is a Filipino sticky rice cake commonly made with glutinous rice, coconut milk and sugar. Ube biko is a purple ube variation. Confirm final ingredient wording and regional context with reliable Filipino cultural or culinary sources before publishing educational copy.

## Luk Chup Trio

### Character direction

Luk Chup is one customer represented by three small friends. Mango is bold and protective, Mangosteen is observant and cautious, and Orange is cheerful and expressive. They speak individually at times, but their emotional arc belongs to the trio.

### Story title

More Than What We Look Like

### Full story

Three tiny customers enter the café together, shining so brightly that Beary first mistakes them for a bowl of fruit that has wandered in by itself.

Mango quickly insists that they are not ordinary fruit. Orange thinks the mistake is funny, but Mangosteen becomes unusually quiet. None of them can explain what they really are.

They remember being carefully shaped and painted. They remember tiny brushes, bright colours and a glossy finish. What they cannot remember is what existed before those colours.

Mango worries that their appearance is only a disguise. Mangosteen wonders whether sharing the same forgotten beginning means none of them are truly unique. Orange tries to keep everyone cheerful, but admits that being admired only for looking cute can feel strangely lonely.

Beary asks them to think about what remains when colour and shape are set aside. The trio listens closely. Beneath the bright fruit skins, each remembers the same soft sweetness. A smooth mung bean centre. Coconut. Sugar. Patient hands turning one simple mixture into many tiny forms.

At first, this makes Mangosteen worry even more. If their centres are alike, are they only copies wearing different costumes?

Beary points out how differently each friend entered the café. Mango protected the others. Mangosteen noticed what everyone else missed. Orange found a reason to laugh. A shared beginning did not erase any of that.

The trio remembers the rest. Their fruit shapes were not meant to hide them. They were a celebration of care, imagination and detail. Each glossy colour invited someone to look closer, while the shared centre kept them connected.

Mango says, "We can come from the same recipe and still grow into ourselves."

Mangosteen finally smiles. Orange announces that being mistaken for fruit is acceptable, as long as they get to surprise people afterward.

Beary realises that identity can be both shared and individual. Knowing what connects us does not make our differences less real.

### Card story

Mango, Mangosteen and Orange feared that their bright shells were only disguises. With Beary's help, they remembered the soft mung bean sweet beneath their glossy colours. They shared one beginning, but every shape still told a different story.

### Card tags

- Thailand
- Sweet
- Mung Bean

### Cultural foundation for later fact-checking

Luk chup are Thai sweets commonly shaped like miniature fruits or vegetables. They are typically made from a mung bean mixture, coloured by hand and finished with a glossy agar coating. Confirm final ingredient and historical wording with reliable Thai cultural or culinary sources before publishing educational copy.

## Pink Songpyeon

### Character direction

Pink Songpyeon is gentle, hopeful and quietly perfectionistic. She hides her curved side because she believes a half-moon shape means that something is missing.

### Story title

The Moon That Wasn't Finished

### Full story

Pink Songpyeon enters the café sideways, carefully keeping her curved edge out of Beary's view.

She remembers fragments of a bright moon, warm hands and the fresh scent of pine. She also remembers being surrounded by many small rice cakes, each shaped a little differently. The memory should feel comforting, but instead it leaves her worried.

Pink Songpyeon believes she was meant to look like a full moon. Her crescent shape feels incomplete. Perhaps someone stopped shaping her too soon. Perhaps the missing part of her memory is also the missing part of herself.

Beary asks whether a moon must always be full to be meaningful. Pink Songpyeon does not answer immediately. She thinks about the night sky and remembers that a crescent is not a broken circle. It is part of a moon that is still changing.

More memories return. Rice dough resting in many palms. Sweet fillings tucked carefully inside. Families sitting together, folding each piece by hand. Pine needles beneath the rice cakes as they steamed, leaving a scent that belonged to the celebration as much as the food itself.

No two pieces looked perfectly alike. Some were neat, some were lopsided and some carried the marks of small hands still learning. Their differences did not spoil the gathering. They made it visible.

Pink Songpyeon finally understands that her shape was never evidence of something missing. The half-moon could carry hope for growth and for good things still to come.

With her memory restored, she turns proudly toward Beary and says, "I wasn't left unfinished. I was shaped like a wish that still has room to grow."

Beary realises that remembering does not always return someone to exactly who they were. Sometimes it reveals who they may still become.

### Card story

Pink Songpyeon feared that her crescent shape meant something was missing. With Beary's help, she remembered warm hands, sweet filling and the scent of pine. She was never unfinished. Like a growing moon, she carried hope for everything still to come.

### Card tags

- Korea
- Tteok
- Filled

### Cultural foundation

Songpyeon are half-moon-shaped rice cakes associated with Chuseok. Families traditionally make them together, add fillings such as sesame seeds, beans or chestnuts, and steam them over pine needles. Educational wording should be checked against current authoritative Korean cultural sources before release.

Reference starting point: https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=224166
