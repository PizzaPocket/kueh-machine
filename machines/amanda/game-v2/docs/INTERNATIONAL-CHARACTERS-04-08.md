## 04. Yakgwa

### Region and tags

- `Korea`
- `Hangwa`
- `Honeyed`

### Chapter title

Sweetness Takes Time

### Arrival and story

Yakgwa arrives polished and cheerful, but keeps checking their reflection in every shiny surface. They remember being shaped like a flower and covered in sweetness, yet they cannot remember why their centre still feels heavy.

Yakgwa admits that they have learned to look ready before they actually feel ready. They worry that if their glaze stops shining, everyone will notice the uncertainty underneath.

Beary helps Yakgwa recover memories of flour, sesame oil, careful frying and a long rest in fragrant syrup. Yakgwa realises that the syrup was never a quick decoration. Its sweetness needed time to travel slowly through every part of them.

### Minigame

Honey Path. Guide syrup through a flower-shaped network without overflowing or skipping any section. Slow, complete paths restore stronger memories than fast ones.

### Restored line

> I thought sweetness was something people saw. I remember now. It had to reach all the way through me.

### Short card story

> Shaped like a flower and soaked in fragrant syrup, Yakgwa once believed they always had to look perfectly polished. They discovered that lasting sweetness is not a surface shine. It develops slowly and reaches every part within.

### Full story

Yakgwa entered Beary's cafe shining like warm amber. They smiled brightly, adjusted each petal and asked whether their glaze still looked smooth.

Behind the careful appearance, Yakgwa felt strangely heavy. They remembered a flower mould and the warmth of oil, but everything after that had disappeared. Without their shine, they feared there might be nothing worth admiring.

Beary helped Yakgwa follow a winding trail of honeyed memories. The path could not be rushed. Syrup moved gradually from one pressed petal to the next, carrying warmth and fragrance deeper each time.

When the final path filled, Yakgwa understood that their sweetness had never been painted onto the surface. It had been given time to settle throughout them. Looking polished was pleasant, but it was not what made them whole.

### Emotional theme

Appearance, patience and inner worth

## 05. Ichigo Daifuku

### Region and tags

- `Japan`
- `Wagashi`
- `Filled`

### Chapter title

More Than One Feeling

### Arrival and story

Ichigo Daifuku arrives feeling confused by the different memories inside them. One is soft and quiet. Another is deep and earthy. A third is bright, sharp and excited.

They worry that these feelings cannot all belong to one person. Whenever the strawberry memory becomes loud, Daifuku tries to hide it beneath the mochi. Whenever the azuki memory feels heavy, they pretend only the sweetness is there.

Beary helps Daifuku rebuild their cross-section in the correct order: soft mochi outside, azuki around the centre and strawberry within. Each part tastes and feels different, yet none needs to disappear.

### Minigame

Inside Out. Fit the mochi shell, azuki layer and strawberry centre together while matching texture and shape clues.

### Restored line

> Soft, deep and bright can all be true at the same time. They are all part of me.

### Short card story

> Ichigo Daifuku holds soft mochi, rich azuki and a bright strawberry in one small sweet. They once tried to choose which feeling was truly theirs, but learned that different emotions can belong together without cancelling one another.

### Full story

Ichigo Daifuku arrived at the cafe carrying three memories that would not agree. The outer memory was soft and calm. The next felt rich and heavy. At the centre was a bright burst that seemed impossible to contain.

Daifuku believed one of these feelings had to be the real one. They tried to stay gentle when they felt excited, and cheerful when a heavier feeling needed attention.

Beary invited Daifuku to rebuild what belonged inside. The mochi shell gave comfort. The azuki layer added depth. The strawberry brought a vivid spark. Each part made room for the next.

When the cross-section was complete, Daifuku saw that being made of contrasts did not make them confused or dishonest. A person could feel soft, serious and joyful, sometimes all in the same moment.

### Emotional theme

Complex emotions and self-acceptance

## 06. Ma'amoul

### Region and tags

- `Middle East`
- `Pastry`
- `Moulded`

### Chapter title

The Message in the Pattern

### Arrival and story

Ma'amoul arrives covered in delicate marks but cannot remember who made them. They have spent so long trying to interpret every line that they no longer notice the filling protected inside.

Ma'amoul fears the pattern is a puzzle with one correct answer. If they misunderstand it, perhaps the memory will be lost forever.

Beary helps Ma'amoul press simple shapes into a mould and compare the impressions. The pattern is not a written instruction. It is the trace of skilled hands, repeated through gatherings and shared tables.

### Minigame

Press and Remember. Rotate mould segments, press them into dough and match the finished pattern to memory fragments.

### Restored line

> I could not read the pattern because it was never a set of instructions. It was the memory of someone's hands.

### Short card story

> Ma'amoul carries a carefully moulded pattern and a sweet filling protected within. They once searched every mark for instructions, but discovered that the pattern was a trace of care, practice and hands working together.

### Full story

Ma'amoul entered Beary's cafe tracing the pattern across their surface. The lines felt familiar, but their meaning remained just out of reach.

They studied every curve as though it were a secret message. The harder they searched for one correct answer, the more distant the memory became.

Beary brought out a wooden mould and a small piece of dough. Together, they pressed, lifted and tried again. Each impression was slightly different, yet every one carried the same patient movement.

Ma'amoul finally remembered a table where many hands shaped sweets together. The pattern was not telling them what to do. It showed that someone had taken the time to make, fill and share them.

### Emotional theme

Tradition, interpretation and shared care

## 07. Hanami Dango

### Region and tags

- `Japan`
- `Wagashi`
- `Skewered`

### Chapter title

Held Together

### Arrival and story

Dango arrives as three colours on one skewer, but the memories keep pulling in different directions. Pink remembers beginning. White remembers the present. Green remembers what continues growing.

The middle Dango feels responsible for keeping everyone together and worries that one wrong movement will make the whole stack fall apart.

Beary helps balance the three dumplings through a gentle rhythm game. Each has a different beat, but the sequence only works when all three are heard.

### Minigame

Three Beat Balance. Tap three simple rhythms in order, then combine them into one calm pattern while keeping the skewer balanced.

### Restored line

> Staying together does not mean moving in exactly the same way. We just need to make room for each other's rhythm.

### Short card story

> Pink, white and green Dango share one skewer while carrying different rhythms. They once believed staying together meant always moving alike, but learned that connection can hold differences without erasing them.

### Full story

Hanami Dango entered the cafe wobbling from side to side. Pink wanted to move quickly. Green preferred to wait. White, caught between them, tried to keep everyone perfectly still.

Each colour remembered something different, and White feared that disagreement meant the skewer was coming apart.

Beary listened to the three rhythms separately. Pink's beat was light and eager. White's was steady. Green's was slow and grounded. When played in sequence, the beats formed a pattern none could make alone.

Dango learned that connection did not require identical thoughts or timing. The skewer gave them support, but listening was what helped them move together.

### Emotional theme

Connection, difference and cooperation

## 08. Taiyaki

### Region and tags

- `Japan`
- `Pastry`
- `Filled`

### Chapter title

Beyond the Mould

### Arrival and story

Taiyaki enters convinced they must know how to swim. They are shaped like a fish, after all. When Beary explains that the cafe has no pond, Taiyaki becomes embarrassed and admits they have been copying what they think a fish should do.

They remember a hot iron mould closing around batter, but not what happened between the two halves. Taiyaki worries that their shape decided their entire identity before they could choose anything.

Beary helps align two mould halves, pour the batter and place the filling so the pastry closes properly. Taiyaki remembers that the fish shape gave them a beginning, not a list of rules.

### Minigame

Match the Mould. Align the two fish-shaped plates, distribute batter and filling evenly, then close them at the right moment.

### Restored line

> I can carry a fish shape without living someone else's idea of a fish.

### Short card story

> Baked in a fish-shaped mould and filled with sweet azuki, Taiyaki once believed their shape decided everything they had to become. They learned that a familiar form can be a beginning while the life inside remains their own.

### Full story

Taiyaki hurried into Beary's cafe and asked where the nearest pond was. They had been practising swimming motions all morning because a fish-shaped pastry should surely know how to swim.

When Beary gently questioned the idea, Taiyaki admitted they did not actually enjoy pretending. They simply believed their mould had already decided what kind of character they should be.

Together, they reconstructed the memory of two heated plates. Batter spread into the fish shape, filling rested at the centre and both halves closed around it. The mould created Taiyaki's outline, but the warmth, filling and experiences inside were not instructions.

Taiyaki left the cafe still proud of their shape. This time, however, they carried it as part of their story rather than a role they were required to perform.

### Emotional theme

Labels, expectations and self-definition
